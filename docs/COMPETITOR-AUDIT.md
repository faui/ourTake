# Meddly and SPORT.VIDEO: public evidence audit

Research date: 9 September 2026. Scope: public product documentation, app-store reviews and release histories, founder posts, customer records, funding announcements, and company-register listings. This is desk research, not a hands-on product benchmark or an audit of private accounts. No competitor was contacted and no paid report was purchased.

## Decision

There is room to compete, but synchronized multi-phone recording and automatic angle switching are already offered. The promising OurFrame position is **a repeatable adult practice session that produces useful personal review and shareable compositions, including selection by wearable data**. Prove that outcome against these products using the same participants and footage.

“My contacts haven't heard of it” is evidence about that network's awareness. It does not establish low paid usage, technical failure, poor retention, or marketing incompetence. The two companies have quite different public footprints.

## 2. What actual reviews and experiences establish

### Meddly

Independent long-form usage evidence was sparse. I did not find a sufficiently large, attributable customer-review corpus to calculate satisfaction, failure prevalence, or retention. Do not replace that missing evidence with SEO review pages for the unrelated Medly music/education/health products or the MEDDLY decision-diagram library.

A November 2024 Reddit founder demo explains the product and seeks testers. A commenter specifically asks whether users can choose which angle appears; the founder says switching is then automated and professional editing export is planned. This is a useful **editorial-control demand signal**, not a paid customer's longitudinal review. The current product advertises EDL export, indicating that the escape hatch is now part of its offer. [Founder conversation](https://www.reddit.com/r/musicians/comments/1gzxhql), [current product](https://meddly.app/).

The iOS changelog reports fixes to device audio input, background upload/retry/progress, camera reliability and quality, and error reporting. Those entries identify maintenance work, not unresolved defects or their incidence. [Version history](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/meddly/id6670494632).

Meddly's Android troubleshooting index covers system termination during recording/upload, incorrect orientation, permission problems, missing saved media, frozen previews, and repeated upload failure. This is a good adversarial test checklist for us; the index alone does not prove every problem occurs frequently. [Troubleshooting](https://meddly.app/docs/devices/troubleshooting-android).

### SPORT.VIDEO

The German App Store exposes four distinct historical reviews relevant to operations: a January 2023 registration-completion button failure; a July 2023 app login failure despite successful website login and password reset; a September 2023 login complaint; and May 2024 praise for stability. Developer replies shown are dated July 2025. These are old anecdotes, not evidence that the current version still fails. The reply dates do not establish private support response times. The page repeats some review text, which I deduplicated. [German reviews](https://apps.apple.com/de/app/sport-video/id1241795100).

The current US listing says its overall rating was reset. Therefore an apparently clean present-day score cannot be read as absence of historical complaints. Recent release notes describe energy/stability improvements, camera shake and tilt guidance, improved scheduled multicam, zoom fixes, and migration of livestream transport from RTMP to SRT. This indicates continued development and a real device-support burden, rather than an abandoned listing. [US listing and releases](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sport-video/id1241795100).

I excluded generic “app not working” troubleshooting templates as customer evidence. One such page reports zero submitted problems; its generated crash/login headings are not incident reports. I also excluded a mirrored allegation about another sports/payment organization because attribution to this product could not be established.

**Lesson:** the clearest observed first-use failures concern identity/onboarding, not sophisticated computer vision. A great renderer is worthless if the second phone cannot join before practice starts.

## 3. Workflow and operating audit

| Stage | Evidence or limitation | OurFrame response |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Meddly's visible community outreach is musician-oriented; SPORT.VIDEO is organization-oriented | Sell through the activity organizer; demonstrate the participant's resulting clip |
| Joining | Historical SPORT.VIDEO auth failures; Meddly teams/roles add concepts | Invitation opens the exact session; preserve context through sign-in/install; test expired links and recovery |
| Setup | Native capture still needs suitable positions and operators | Saved venue layouts; one protected wide view; visible preflight evidence |
| Recording | Both have native apps, yet reliability work continues | Owner stop, interruptions, free-space and thermal handling are product features |
| Network | Meddly requires connectivity for coordinated commands; locally saved recording is separate | Control transport failure must not destroy an ongoing take |
| Editorial quality | Meddly automated selection prompted requests for control | “Keep this angle,” exclude clip, protected audio, and editable selection before expensive render |
| Metering | Multicamera acquisition increases cost even for a short final reel | Quote money for the whole session and requested outputs before recording/rendering |
| Distribution | SPORT.VIDEO channels and sponsor value address a business buyer | Venue/coach pays; players receive value without all becoming subscribers |
| Retention | Neither public sources establish cohort retention | Measure second and fourth unattended sessions, not registrations or compliments |

Meddly explicitly states that its media pipeline does **not use AI**. It documents local recording, internet-mediated coordinated commands, subsequent uploads, and limited APIs/integrations. Its FAQ also notes power-saving modes can impair uploading. The engineering lesson is to distinguish automatic editing from semantic understanding. We should compete on useful selections and reliability, not the word “AI.” [FAQ](https://meddly.app/faq).

SPORT.VIDEO's multicam flow associates devices with the same competition/teams/game, permits staggered starts, and consolidates views; this is not proof of exposure-level synchronization or autonomous player-specific editing. Each camera consumes a game allowance, and offline highlights can upload later. A canonical session/take identifier is preferable to matching names in our own data model. [Multicam guide](https://sport.video/multicam).

SPORT.VIDEO pricing documentation describes separate game/camera and livestream/watch-hour allowances. One viewer watching an hour uses one watch-hour. It also documents cancellation-before-downgrade and a 60% annual discount. The current numeric plan-card prices were not exposed in the retrieved page, so I do not invent them. **Inference:** multiple meters and seasonal commitments can complicate purchase and support; no retrieved customer review measures that effect. Our first paid pilot should have one comprehensible session allowance and an upfront overage quote. [Pricing rules](https://product.sport.video/pricing/).

Meddly charges approximately $0.06 per source-camera minute and $0.50 per output minute through team tokens. Its pricing page states unprocessed uploads expire after seven days, download requires processing, and platform processing errors receive token refunds. These policies expose both cost discipline and possible trust friction. Our originals should remain exportable, retention dates visible, and failed renders should not consume payment. [Pricing](https://meddly.app/pricing).

A company can persist as a modest niche business with low public awareness. Conversely, claimed reach can coexist with weak margins. Neither product's public evidence settles profitability. Investor counts and headcount directories are poor substitutes for a P&L.

## 5. Marketing failure or technology failure?

My diagnosis is **distribution and workflow constraints, with real technical maintenance costs; evidence is insufficient to label either company a failure**.

- Meddly: small visible acquisition footprint, broad event positioning, and an organizer-dependent workflow. Limited awareness could reflect a small operation, an early app, or low repeat demand. A causal conclusion requires its activation and retention funnel.
- SPORT.VIDEO: regionally concentrated organizational distribution and embedded partner branding plausibly explain low US consumer recognition. Public history supports niche adoption. Historical auth complaints show preventable friction, but do not establish that the recording technology failed.
- Category-wide hypothesis: the beneficiary is often playing, while someone else must set up, monitor, retrieve, and pay. This coordination burden can suppress adoption even when the software works.
- Native software resolves some camera constraints; it does not automatically supply an operator, working venue network, good viewpoint, or desired edit.

Our opportunity is to remove repeated work: venue remembers the layout, members join the existing group, session opens from a booking/QR, a protected camera records safely, and each player receives useful moments. The behavioral innovation is **personal retrieval from a collective practice record**, potentially triggered by physical effort, rather than another destination social network.

## 6. Lessons to incorporate from the beginning

1. One session link and one organizer; viewer access stays on the web. Do not make every viewer install the capture app.
2. Demonstrate a ten-second test capture and playable upload receipt before trusting a game to the system.
3. Distinguish connected, armed, recording, saved locally, uploaded, and verified. A heartbeat is not evidence of recorded video.
4. Preserve full source media until explicit retention/cleanup criteria pass. Avoid silent expiry and surprise download paywalls.
5. Keep one wide master and uninterrupted reference audio. Automatic zoom/angle selection must be reversible where possible.
6. Show source-camera minutes, expected output cost, and failure refund policy in ordinary currency.
7. Prioritize historical first-use failure cases: sign-in context loss, permissions, app/web identity mismatch, link expiry, interruptions, and upload recovery.
8. Include heart-rate query, timeline correction, and manual subject tagging before complex autonomous recognition.
9. Make the venue's second session faster than the first. Track organizer minutes and failed sessions per 100 attempts.
10. Measure repeat paid use and support burden separately from views, downloads, and created teams.

## 7. Next evidence collection

Run matched trials on both apps and OurFrame with consenting adults: one short dance take, one sports drill, and one longer rehearsal on the same devices. Record time-to-join, steps, permissions, successful camera minutes, recovery after network interruption, upload completion, cost, time-to-first-playable, editorial corrections, and whether participants voluntarily reuse/share the result. Separate clean-network tests from fault injection. Retain originals outside each tested app.

Recruit five current and five lapsed users of each product if reachable, with no sales pitch: last event used, frequency, who filmed, who paid, last failure, abandoned step, export quality, alternative used, reason for returning/stopping. Interviews have not been performed and no outreach has been sent.

Ask the companies for definitions and ranges: active recording organizations in 90 days, paying accounts, cameras/session, repeat rate, average ticket, acquisition source, refund rate, support minutes, and partner concentration. The first priority for paid financial research would be the actual Sportvideos365 filing, while recognizing its disclosure limits.

## Evidence hygiene

App listings and company pages are snapshots, not global measures. Company testimonials are not independent interviews. Release notes establish work performed, not bug prevalence. Directory placeholders and AI-generated company descriptions were excluded: one Sportvideos365 directory result mixed the historic business with casino-related content. The rival MEDDLY software library and Medly brands were excluded. All revenue scenarios above are my assumptions and calculations.

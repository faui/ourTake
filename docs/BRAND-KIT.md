# ourTake — brand and interface system v0.2

Updated 10 September 2026. **This is the authoritative brand reference.** The founder selected **ourTake**, including this capitalization, and approved the principles below. Earlier OurFrame / OurTake / takeO naming recommendations are superseded. Runtime code, infrastructure names, and existing pilot assets still use OurFrame; migration is separate work. No trademark/domain clearance is claimed.

## Decision discipline

- **Agreed:** name, meaning, priority order, shared creative play, capture-to-discovery narrative, numbered takes for compositions, and evaluation across four surfaces.
- **Exploratory:** every new mark, palette, letterform, motion treatment, slogan, and vocabulary asset in the linked concept board. No visual winner is selected. The creative plaything is the leading hypothesis, not an approved logo.
- Keep enduring brand decisions here; keep visual specimens in `docs/brand-exploration/`. Update this document when a direction is accepted rather than creating competing strategy documents. Dated research remains historical.
- Agents must read this kit before identity/copy/UI work. Preserve the exact name, ordered principles, product truth, and distinction between agreed and exploratory. Do not silently promote concept assets to production or mix competing families into one identity.

## Position

**Brand essence: shared creative play.** ourTake brings our moments and perspectives together, giving us new ways to create, play, and relive them.

Desired perception: **“This looks beautifully made, easy to enjoy, and worth bringing my people into.”** Togetherness gives the brand its specific emotional purpose. Play includes both social enjoyment and the satisfying manipulation of a toy or gadget. Craft must make participation approachable. The collective camera remains a useful product description, not the whole brand definition.

Name logic: **our** = our people and shared contribution; **take** = a recording, a perspective, and another interpretation. Understanding the English wordplay must not be necessary to enjoy the product. Brand narrative: **Capture a take → bring together our takes → discover another take.**

Product description: ourTake brings your group's cameras into one private session. Record from complementary angles, collect the footage, and make compositions for the whole group or moments featuring you. Shared contributions need not imply unanimous opinions or identical outputs.

Candidate copy (not a selected master tagline): “Our moments. Our take.” / “One moment. Many takes.” / “Make room for another angle.” / “See what else is in our moment.” The earlier “Every angle. Together.” remains a candidate, not a promise of complete camera coverage.

Pilot wording must stay accurate: guided camera placement, coordinated recording, tagged personal edits. Do not advertise automatic court calibration, AI player recognition, or Bluetooth frame synchronization before those exist and pass tests.

## Core tenets — agreed priority order

| Priority | Tenet | Intended response / design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Togetherness and belonging | “This is something for us.” Relationships among parts, shared spaces, and complementary perspectives. |
| 2 | Playful invitation | “I want to try that.” Social joy plus the satisfaction of turning, joining, sliding, or recombining a well-made object. |
| 3 | Distinctive, memorable character | “I know that's ourTake.” A specific silhouette or typographic gesture that can accumulate recognition from launch. |
| 4 | Creative transformation | “We can make something surprising.” Reframe and recombine familiar material; innovation expressed through behavior. |
| 5 | Artistic craft | “Someone cared about this.” Deliberate proportion, spacing, curves, and rhythm; accessible to non-designers. |
| 6 | Global accessibility | “This feels accessible to me.” No dependence on English puns or culturally specific gestures; research actual interpretations. |
| 7 | Contemporary, enduring expression | “This feels fresh.” A present-day digital identity whose character survives changes in fashion. |

These rank expressive priorities, not permission to compromise accessibility. All candidates must pass small-size recognition, legibility, contrast, monochrome reproduction, touch/keyboard use, reduced motion, and cross-market interpretation checks. A brand new symbol should be paired with **ourTake** and plain product context in invitations and first encounters; familiarity cannot be assumed.

## Composition naming

**Agreed direction:** compositions from one capture session are numbered takes with descriptive suffixes, continuing the filmmaking/interpretation association.

Working presentation convention: `Take 1 — The whole story`, `Take 2 — Little details`, `Take 3 — Everyone's angle`; filename form `take1-the-whole-story.mp4`. Treat typography, separator, and these example descriptors as proposals, not fixed product taxonomy.

Implementation recommendation: assign the number within its session when a composition is created; retain it through title edits and deletions; failed retries of the same composition do not create another creative take. Internal immutable IDs remain separate. Numbers indicate alternatives, never quality or camera identity. A session contains source recordings and derived takes; do not call every camera's upload “Take 1” and confuse it with a finished composition. Display actual generation status separately. Validate numbering across collaborators before implementing it.

### Method and evaluation

For each family: thesis → strongest recognizable gesture → derived visual grammar → four contextual treatments → critique. Compare families using identical sample content, copy, and layout constraints; color is secondary. Each family must work in (1) app icon, (2) a friend's invitation, (3) finished photo/video composition, and (4) short motion sequence. Include wordmark context, actual 16/24/48 px samples, monochrome, portrait/landscape composition, and reduced-motion end states. The board is an initial design probe, not evidence that audience tests passed.

After selection, refine one family and test recall after a brief exposure, first-impression meaning, mistaken brand/category associations, legibility of “ourTake,” and invitation comprehension with intended adult users across target markets. Record observations rather than invented preference scores. Distinctiveness/clearance, real-device icon review, and photo/video background testing remain open.

Decorative seams must yield to real content and never imply verified synchronization or output readiness.

**C / Negative-space study 03 — O and T, exploratory.** Founder suggested exploring the initials within the weave; this is permission to investigate, not selection of a monogram. Three probes: (1) **Shared counter:** O-like circular enclosure, T-shaped transparent aperture; strongest negative-space idea but can read as a T badge. ### Candidate visual vocabulary

Common semantic grammar: **contribution → gathering → reframing → another take**. Family A expresses this with brackets; B with joining pieces; C with interwoven strands. Each sheet explores a contribution unit, joining rule, shared form, and repeat pattern. Use family-native forms for invitation graphics, composition selectors, empty states, editorial crops, website dividers, and completion signatures; keep familiar operational controls legible. No symbolic substitution for record/stop, permission, errors, or privacy controls.

Across families: pair color with shape/text, preserve real media color, keep decorative geometry away from faces/action, use small attribution rather than large compulsory watermarks, and allow exploratory artwork to yield to the content. A logo need not depict every brand tenet. Do not combine an aperture, people, play triangle, sparkle, and initials into an explanatory rebus.

## Existing pilot implementation — retained pending visual selection

The following asset, tokens, typography, and interface guidance describe the **current OurFrame pilot**, not the chosen future ourTake identity. Do not replace operational colors, assets, or components with concept candidates until a family is selected and integration is requested.

### Identity assets

`public/ourframe-mark.svg` is the scalable frame-and-dot mark. It uses a charcoal rounded square and lime capture corners, with a central shared moment. The interface wordmark is lowercase `ourframe` with a lime dot. Maintain clear space of at least one dot diameter around the mark. Do not distort, animate it continuously, or put it over a busy video without a solid backing.

## Color tokens

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | `#0D1011` | Primary background |
| Studio surface | `#151B1D` | Panels and editing controls |
| Elevated surface | `#191F21` | Dialogs and menus |
| Frame lime | `#D8FC71` | Primary action, selection, ready status |
| Primary text | `#F2F4EE` | Headings and body |
| Secondary text | `#ACB5B7` | Descriptions and secondary metadata |
| Border | `#343C3E` | Structure and separation |
| Recording | `#FD797C` | Recording/stop affordance |
| Pending | `#E5B968` | Offline/not-ready status |

Use lime as an intentional action color rather than decorating every surface. Pair states with text; color alone must not carry readiness, recording, or errors. Video stays faithful to the source rather than tinted by the product brand.

## Typography

The pilot uses a local Arial/Helvetica/system sans stack for reliable offline loading. Headings use medium weight, tight but readable letter spacing, and a limited scale: 64/44/32/24/20 px depending on context and viewport. Body text is 16 px; regular controls and explanatory labels target 14–16 px. Dense secondary metadata uses 12–13 px. Monospace is reserved for timing and occasional compact category labels.

If replacing the font later, choose a licensed modern grotesk, self-host WOFF2 files, and retest line breaks, phone scaling, and numeric timecodes. A font subscription should not block field validation.

## Layout and interaction

- Start with a working surface: create or join a session immediately.
- Desktop capture: large viewfinder with a narrow setup panel. Phone capture: viewfinder first, then placement guidance.
- Tabs separate Capture, Footage, Create, and Crew. The capture component remains mounted when changing tabs so navigation does not silently stop recording.
- Primary touch controls are approximately 44–48 px high. Put record/stop actions in predictable positions.
- Cards use 12–24 px corner radii and thin borders. Avoid excessive nested cards and decorative charts.
- Use a functional court diagram to communicate selected camera positions, explicitly not a measured spatial map.
- Loading states report actual upload/render progress. No fabricated percentages for unspecified work.
- Critical errors remain until dismissed. Confirmation toasts may expire.
- Dialogs use the starter's accessible primitives. Keyboard focus is visible; reduced-motion preferences disable nonessential motion.
- Sources and final editions use real video players. Empty states explain the next useful action and do not contain fictitious games, participants, or successful renders.

## Four edition recipes

| Edition | Treatment | Editorial intent |
|---|---|---|
| Original | Natural color, approximately five-second shots | Balanced social memory |
| Pulse | Approximately 2.5-second shots, slight saturation/contrast lift | A brisk, shareable sequence |
| Noir | Monochrome, restrained contrast | A visually consistent alternate edition |
| Study | Longer source intervals, natural presentation | Movement context and review |

These are deterministic recipes in the pilot. They are not trained style models, music-beat detection, or equivalent to the full CapCut/TikTok editing catalog. Exports retain actual source frames. Portrait output fits the whole source view over a soft background so an arbitrary center crop does not cut players out.

## Copy principles

Use direct actions: “Enable camera,” “My framing is ready,” “Start all cameras,” “Mark moment,” “Tag me,” “Download MP4.” Distinguish “my camera” from “featuring me.” Keep infrastructure vocabulary out of the normal flow except where it explains a real setup or connection failure. Do not pressure people into installing the app or sharing more material than the session needs.

## Naming validation

The masterbrand decision is **ourTake**; do not reopen the earlier candidate-name contest by default. Test how people say, spell, and recall it, and whether invitations communicate the product. Trademark/domain clearance remains outstanding. Existing OurFrame labels identify implementation debt rather than an active competing brand choice.
